How it works

The walking network is the crossing detector.

No one surveyed these roads. If Amazon Location can only find a long way round between two stops a few metres apart, the street has no safe place to cross there, or the map is missing one.

How a gap is scored

Two stops on either side of a divided road. The straight line is short. The walked route goes to the next place a pedestrian can cross.

3×The walk is at least three times the straight-line gap
+150…and more than 150 metres longer than the gap
↑Ranked by extra minutes × buses a day at the pair

  1. Pair the stops

    The BMTC GTFS feed has 9,960 stops. Stops 12–70 m apart that do not share a parent station are treated as the two sides of one road: 2,587 pairs.

    BMTC GTFS · ODbLPython

  2. Walk between them

    For the 962 pairs with 400+ buses a day, a pedestrian CalculateRoutes call walks from one stop to the other. 285 come back as gaps. Every response is cached, so a rerun repeats no paid calls.

    Amazon Location · Routes

  3. Name the dangerous places

    Junctions and corridors from the 2023 Traffic Police report are geocoded. Corridors become car routes between their endpoints. The “stay off” walks pass each corridor as an avoid area.

    Amazon Location · PlacesRoutes · avoid corridor

  4. Join in Athena

    Pairs and blackspots are staged on S3. One query attaches the nearest named place within 500 m and writes the Iceberg table safe_crossing.crossings.

    Amazon AthenaApache IcebergAmazon S3

  5. Ship a map that cannot fail

    The Athena export becomes static GeoJSON behind CloudFront. MapLibre draws it on the Amazon Location basemap. Clicking a stop never waits on a live API call.

    Amazon Location · MapsCloudFrontMapLibre GL
